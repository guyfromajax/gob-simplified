**All 55 anchors are inside their own polygon: confirmed `True`.** This is the property the pole buys: a centroid of the concave rings (`ZONE_131_NORMAL["C"]`, the 2-3 baseline zones) can land outside the region, and then every clamp and every pull is measured from a point the defender may not legally stand on.

**Two clearances worth flagging now rather than after the weights are chosen.** `ZONE_131_{LOWER,LOWER_CORNER}_SHIFT["SF"]` has a clearance of **0.6** and `ZONE_131_{UPPER,UPPER_CORNER}_SHIFT["SG"]` **0.5** — these are the three-spot sliver zones (area 8.5). Their anchors are legal but the polygon is barely wider than a point, so those defenders are effectively pinned regardless of weights: the clamp will refuse almost any travel. That is the v1 "stay in your own area" rule working as specified, but it means the sink cannot do anything for those two defenders. It is a consequence of the spot lists, not of the model.

**Role classes derived from geometry: 9 perimeter, 30 spanning, 16 interior**, from the anchor's rim distance alone against thresholds picked off the measured distribution (quartiles 7.7 / 13.6 / 16.0 / 20.0 / 22.9), not chosen a priori. There are **no per-zone constants** in `zone_sink.py`.

## The model as built

```
position = clamp_into_own_polygon( anchor + pull_toward_ball + pull_toward_basket )
```

- **Primary term — ball distance and side.** `strongness = 1 − min(1, |ball.y − anchor.y| / 30)`, continuous rather than a binary side flag so a defender anchored near the midline does not flip between two positions as the ball crosses. The ball and basket weights both interpolate on it: strong side shades toward the ball, weak side sinks toward the rim. Both pulls are proportional to the actual distance (anchor→ball, anchor→rim), so "how far the ball is from his area" is in the magnitude, not just the direction.
- **Secondary term — role class** sets `reach`, the cap on total travel from the anchor. Three numbers, one per class.
- **Clamp** binary-searches back along anchor→target until inside the ring. The anchor is interior by construction, so this always terminates inside the defender's own area. v1 does not let him leave it.

### Tunable constants

| constant | value | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `INTERIOR_RIM_DISTANCE` | 12.0 | anchor closer than this to the rim classes as `interior` |
| `PERIMETER_RIM_DISTANCE` | 20.0 | anchor further than this classes as `perimeter`; between the two is `spanning` |
| `SIDE_SPAN` | 30.0 | y-distance at which a defender counts as fully weak side |
| `reach_perimeter` / `reach_spanning` / `reach_interior` | per preset | how far that class may leave its anchor at all |
| `ball_strong` / `ball_weak` | per preset | fraction of the anchor→ball distance pulled, by side |
| `basket_strong` / `basket_weak` | per preset | fraction of the anchor→rim distance pulled, by side |
| `GOB_ZONE_SINK` | `0` | master flag, **default OFF** |
| `GOB_ZONE_SINK_WEIGHTS` | `balanced` | which preset is active |

This was not something I expected. Today's menu pick routinely sends two defenders to the *same listed spot* — the `midLane` parking noted in the earlier report is the same phenomenon. The sink gives each defender a distinct anchor, so they separate.

**2. The one stack the sink cannot fix is a data property, not a model flaw.** `ZONE_23_UPPER_SHIFT["PG"]` and `["PF"]` are different spot lists that nevertheless have the **same pole of inaccessibility, (84.75, 39.75)**. The sink is deterministic given the ring, so those two defenders land on exactly the same point whenever both their zones are empty. It is the only anchor collision in all 55. I have **not** fixed it: it is a spot-list question (those two zones overlap heavily on the upper side), the lists were just approved, and this brief scopes me to consume them. `_apply_multi_defender_offsets` will de-stack them at render in many cases — it fires on ~70% of empty-branch steps — but that is a cosmetic backstop, not a defensive decision. ## The IQ hook — wired, neutral

`_zone_sink_iq_error(defender_pos)` in `shared_defense.py` **returns 0.0 unconditionally and draws nothing**, so enabling `GOB_ZONE_SINK` alone is draw-neutral against the flag being off and the geometry can be tuned before attribute variance is layered on, exactly as the brief asks.

The shape it will take is documented in place rather than left to be re-derived:

- The error is **directional, not a magnitude**: a positive error multiplies the ball weight up (`w_ball *= 1 + err`) and the basket weight down (`w_basket *= 1 − err`). A low-IQ defender is pulled *too far toward the ball* and gives up his help toward the rim; a high-IQ defender holds the balance. That is why it is applied to the weights and not to the travel distance.
- It must be rolled **once per defender per zone possession**, not per step. The docstring records the arithmetic: ~5 rolls per zone possession, **~282 a game on a ~69,000-draw base, ~0.4%** — against ~11,200 a game if rolled per step, which the brief rules out. Stage B must confirm the measured figure against that prediction.
- Team `defensive_efficiency` scales the unit's overall discipline by multiplying the rolled error, and the docstring names that as the wiring point.
