## 1. One loader after a game

**Where the two came from.**

| Screen | Source | When |
|---|---|---|
| Team banner, "Simulating Computer Games", rotating player stat lines | `PageLoadOverlay.show({ variant: 'pulse', … })` with `buildPostgameStatFeed`, called by the "Go To Locker Room" click in `js/phaser/utils/gameCompletionPopup.js` (the popup at the end of a game) and by the same button on `box-score.js` | While `/franchise/complete-week/phase-b` (the computer games) runs. The click handler then **hid** the overlay and navigated. |
| Spinning basketball | The Office's own first-paint loader: static markup in `franchise-command-center.html`, lifted by `hideFccLoadingOverlay()` when the page is drawn | From the Office document's first paint until it is ready |

**Fix.** Both "Go To Locker Room" handlers now show the plain spinner (same image, same solid fill as the Office's) and do not hide it when the week finishes: it stays up through the navigation, and the Office's spinner takes over in the same place. It comes down on the old page only when the week did not finish (the existing alert, the player stays put). Nothing in sim, finalize, `cpu_week_pool` or `sim_rng` changed; the phase-b call is the same call.

**Test.** `fcc-fresh-after-game.spec.js` plays the real flow (Office → lineup → sim → popup → locker room) and records what the loader shows on every frame across both documents. The sequence must be exactly: court no loader → court spinner → Office spinner → Office drawn. On the old code it is court pulse → court nothing → Office spinner, and the test fails.

**Left as it is:** the Office's recovery path (`recoverCpuSimsBeforeFccRender`, the "Finishing Week" banner with a progress count) still uses the pulse. It only runs when a week was left half-finished (closed tab, crash), not after a normal game. See question 1.

## 4. Schedule scores

**Cause.** The result column had a fixed width (72px) set when it held only a score. It now holds the W/L plate and a link with its own padding, which is wider than the column, so the score ran past the card's edge and was clipped. On the real save at 1280 "26-19" lost its last digit; a three-digit score lost more.

**Fix.** The result column now takes the width of its widest score and the team column takes what is left, so a long opponent name gives way (ellipsis) and the score never does. The score also keeps 10px from the card's border, matching the week number on the left.

**Test.** At 1280, 1920 and 2000, with three-digit scores on both sides and ranked long names: every score's text is inside its card with at least 8px to spare on both sides, inside its own cell, not under the team name, and the pixels at both ends of the score belong to the score. Checked on the real scratch save as well.
