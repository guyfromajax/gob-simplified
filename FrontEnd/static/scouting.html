SCOUTING

Before every game, a Scouting Report appears in your Command Center. It's your best resource to learn about your upcoming opponent.

The Habit

Scouting should be one of your first steps each week. It's best to scout before you run training, as what you learn about your opponent could shape how you prepare.

The Team Page

The Scouting Report gives you the projected five, but games aren't won by five players. Follow the link to the opponent's team page for the full picture. Here you'll find every player's full attributes and season statistics. The attributes tell you who they are; the season stats tell you what they've actually done. Together they tell you a complete story about your opponent's team makeup.

Turning Intel Into a Plan

Read Their Tendencies

Look at their play usage and team measures and ask:

Do they lean on one play more than the rest?
Do they favor Motion or Set Plays on offense?
Do they run more Man or Zone on defense?
Do they like to Fast Break, Press, or Trap more than a typical team?
Who's their top scorer — or scorers — that you'll need to account for?

Find Your Mismatches

Look at their projected five and the bench players who get real minutes, and ask where you hold an edge:

Do you have an advantage down low that inside plays could exploit?
Do you have the depth to push the pace and fatigue them with Fast Breaks or Presses & Traps?
Do they lack the athletes to defend an attack-heavy scheme with lots of drives to the basket?

The above are just a small sample of a nearly infinite number of questions and angles you can consider while scouting. As you play the game and get a feel for your team's identity, you'll figure out which scouting approach works best for you.
