
**Momentum Basics**
- All players have momentum attributes that range from -5 to +5.
- Momentum shifts happen during gameplay, for the most part. There are a small number of instances where your training might create a slight increase or decrease in player momentum before tipoff, but these are very rare.
- Each player's momentum is always reset to zero at the end of the game.
- Only players have momentum attributes; teams do not.
- The Team Momentum bar on the gameplay screen is an aggregate of the five active players' current momentum values. It scales from -25 to +25.
- You can see each player's current momentum value during gameplay by hovering your cursor over the player's name in the Team Box Score section. The bar scales from -5 to +5.

**Momentum Shifting Instances**
- Note these instances are not straight one-to-one. One instance of these does not necessarily increase or decrease a player's momentum by 1. But know that these are the types of moments that can give a player a momentum boost or nerf, generally speaking.
- Positive Moments
    - Consecutive made shots: note this is just relative to himself. If a player goes on a run over the course of the game where he doesn't miss any shots, even if they're not consecutive made shots for his team (i.e. his teammates can also be making shots during this run), his momentum will increase.
    - And-1 situations -- when a player makes a shot on which he's fouled.
    - Offensive Rebounds.
    - Made Free Throws.
    - Game-changing defensive plays like steals, blocks, and drawing a charge.
    - Making the final shot of a quarter.
- Negative Moments
    - Consecutive missed shots (same consecutive logic applies here).
    - Missed Free Throws.
    - Committing a turnover.
    - Getting a shot blocked.
    - Committing a charge (this gets inside the player's head and makes him more reluctant to aggressively drive to the basket).

**Momentum Impact**
- Positive Momentum
    - Players more likely to make shot attempts.
    - Players more likely to make free throw attempts.
    - Players more likely to make game-changing defensive plays.
    - Players fatigue less.
    - The team is less likely to commit a shot clock violation when the aggregate momentum of all active players is positive.
- Negative Momentum
    - Players less likely to make shot attempts.
    - Players less likely to make free throws.
    - Players less likely to make game-changing defensive plays.
    - The team is more likely to commit a shot clock violation when the aggregate momentum of all active players is negative.

**Breaks Impact On Momentum**
- Breaks in gameplay have different levels of impact on player momentum. All breaks tend to push the player's momentum back to 0. So if a player has positive momentum, it will tend to decrease, and if he has negative momentum, it will tend to increase.
- Halftime breaks have the largest impact, followed closely by quarter breaks.
- Timeouts also have an impact, but less so than halftime and quarter breaks.
- Note -- you can use timeouts to attempt to cool down an opponent's player who is red hot, or calm down any of your players who may be ice cold.
