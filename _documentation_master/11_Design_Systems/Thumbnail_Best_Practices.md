# GOB YouTube Thumbnail Brief
*Geeked-Out Basketball · researched Oct 1, 2026 · goal: Steam wishlists ahead of the March 2027 launch*

---

## (a) Top 10 principles (with sources)

1. **Packaging is the promise, and YouTube scores it on watch time, not clicks.** Test & Compare picks the winner by *watch time share*, not CTR, and YouTube says it chose watch time on purpose so that clickbait doesn't win. Their own description of clickbait: "high CTR but low average view duration and lower than expected impressions." → For GOB, the smart-scout voice isn't a handicap. It's how the system grades you anyway. [YT Help: A/B test](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en) · [YT Help: Impressions & CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)
2. **Be accurate, and deliver in the first minute.** YouTube's guidance says misleading thumbnails hurt watch time and discoverability. The MrBeast production guide says the title and thumbnail set expectations that the first minute has to confirm. [YouTube Creators: Titles & Thumbnails](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubFTkoJkNX4) · [MrBeast production guide (PDF)](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)
3. **One idea per thumbnail, readable at a glance.** Paddy Galloway's "glance test": 1–3 elements, empty space is fine, and don't fill the frame just because it's there. YouTube also warns against busy designs. [Galloway on Jay Clouse (transcript)](https://pickscribe.com/v/HEiAiPGatwc/) · [YT Help: Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300)
4. **Few words.** In vidIQ's study of 500 breakout videos (Jul 2026), thumbnails that had text used a median of 5 words, and most used 3 or fewer. Thumbnail Bench's study of 11,223 covers from 100K+ channels found a median of 4 words overall, 3 in Gaming, and 33% of Gaming covers had no text at all (this is an AI-tagged study from a vendor, so treat it as directional). [vidIQ study](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/) · [vidIQ tips](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/) · [Thumbnail Bench study](https://thumbnailbench.com/resources/youtube-thumbnail-study-2026) → **GOB rule: 0–4 words, 3 is the target.**
5. **A clear face helps. An exaggerated face isn't needed.** Faces appeared on 69% of vidIQ's breakouts and 80% of the top 50, but exaggerated expressions showed up on only about 5%. Thumbnail Bench found "shocked" on just 11% of faces, while neutral (35%) and happy (32%) were most common. Gaming channels use faces less (47%). → Jamie's face can carry a thumbnail with a thoughtful or skeptical "scout" look. No "shocked" faces. [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/) · [NetInfluencer on Thumbnail Bench](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-thumbnail-study-of-100k-channels-finds-shocked-faces-on-just-1-in-10-covers/)
6. **Use either a face or high contrast.** 89% of vidIQ's breakouts had one or both. Without a face, contrast and one bold focal object have to do the work. GOB's near-black base with orange is a natural high-contrast pair. [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/)
7. **Rule of thirds, designed for mobile first.** Use 1280×720 (16:9). Put the main subject on a thirds intersection and make sure it still reads when shrunk. Uploads below 720p get every test variant downscaled to 480p. [YouTube Creators: Titles & Thumbnails](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubFTkoJkNX4) · [YT Help: A/B test](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
8. **The thumbnail shows, the title tells, and they shouldn't repeat each other.** Titles should be short with the key words first, and branding or episode numbers go at the end. Galloway: the title and thumbnail should "pose a question… only answer[ed] by clicking." [YT Help tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300) · [Galloway, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_i-just-turned-29-and-realised-ive-spent-activity-7327726416354037772-jvX0)
9. **"Click to unpause."** Show a scene in the middle of the action (mid-sentence, mid-decision) rather than a posed one. Galloway's examples: someone mid-talk with mouth open beats someone smiling at the camera. For GOB, that could be Jamie mid-explanation pointing at a grade, or a trade screen just before the confirm click. [Galloway, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_what-do-these-four-viral-youtube-videos-have-activity-7444402853705854978-IsL1)
10. **Make several concepts and keep iterating.** Galloway's advice is 3+ thumbnails and 10+ titles per upload. YouTube says to shoot extra stills during filming, keep up with what works in your community, and update older thumbnails. [Galloway, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_i-just-turned-29-and-realised-ive-spent-activity-7327726416354037772-jvX0) · [YT Help tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300)

**Discovery-stage notes (small channel):**
- **Browse vs. Suggested vs. Search.** YouTube ranks each video against everything else a viewer could watch. A low CTR on a lot of impressions often just means YouTube is testing the video with a broader audience. Small, loyal audiences push CTR up. The MrBeast/Viewstats "Chucky" and YouTube's Todd Beaupré discussion covers how Home and Watch Next are ranked separately ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s); [CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). How that plays out for GOB:
  - **Search** viewers already know the query (for example, a "basketball GM game" type search). Use readable keywords and a clear game visual.
  - **Suggested** viewers are next to FM, OOTP, or 2K MyNBA videos. Look familiar enough to fit the niche (face plus UI or crest) but stand out in color. FM thumbnails lean pink, purple, and blue, so GOB's orange on near-black is easy to spot next to them.
  - **Browse** needs the strongest single hook.
- **Consistency vs. breaking it.** Galloway suggests a quota: roughly 1 in 3 to 1 in 5 videos try something new instead of repeating what already works ([interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dAR3d6xnG0o)). For GOB: keep a fixed brand frame (the 70/30 approach below) and break it on purpose for each milestone and every 4th video.

## (c) Three GOB layout formulas

**Fixed for every thumbnail (the 70%):** #0b0d14 base, at least one #F79420 accent, Bebas Neue Pro for display text, a small "GOB" corner mark top-left (no wordmark lockup bigger than about 8% of the frame). Keep the bottom-right ~15% clear because YouTube's duration stamp sits there. Inter is used only for small labels, if at all. Never use more than 2 accent colors in one thumbnail.

### 1. Dev Diary ("Founder at the whiteboard")
- **Composition:** Jamie in the right third, chest-up, face about 30% of the frame height, caught mid-explanation (mouth slightly open or pointing). Left two-thirds: **one** real build artifact, either a single UI panel cropped tight or a before/after split. Small series badge top-left: `DEV DIARY #07` (Inter Bold on an orange chip).
- **Text:** 1–3 words, Bebas Neue Pro, about 18% of frame height, in white or #FFD700, naming the *stakes or problem*, not the topic. "TRADE AI", "WE CUT IT", "TOO EASY?". The title does the explaining ("Why our trade AI kept fleecing you — Dev Diary #7").
- **Color:** near-black base, orange rim light or underline. Light blue #4A90D9 only to highlight UI.
- **Focal point:** Jamie's eyeline or hand points toward the artifact. The viewer's eye goes face → artifact → text.
- **Voice check:** curious or skeptical expression, a question rather than a shout. No red arrows or fake shock. A hand-drawn orange circle on one stat is fine.

### 2. Gameplay Breakdown ("The Scout's Report")
- **Composition:** One hero object centered or on the left third: a giant **letter grade** (A++ / D–) or a player card, ideally next to a team banner. Optional: a Jamie cutout in the corner at about 20% for continuity (try both with and without in a test). Or Coach Sammy reacting.
- **Text:** 0–3 words. Often the grade *is* the text ("A++" with "REALLY?" underneath). If there's a number, it's the hook (GMTK style).
- **Color:** the grade gets a meaning color: green #34EC27 for elite, #FFD700 for good, orange for a warning. Dark blue #27408E court-line texture in the background at low opacity.
- **Focal point:** the grade or card takes up at least 35% of the frame and has to be readable at 160 px wide.
- **Voice check:** a challenge the viewer can test themselves against ("Your rookie grades are wrong"), not hype.

### 3. Announcement / Milestone ("Key Art Moment")
- **Composition:** GOB key art or Coach Sammy as the hero, with the event label on a clean bar (Paradox style): `STEAM NEXT FEST`, `DEMO OUT`, `RELEASE DATE`. Jamie optional (a candid shot of him reacting can work, like Paradox's dev videos).
- **Text:** 2–4 words, all in the label. The date or number in #FFD700 ("MARCH 2027").
- **Color:** this is the one format that **intentionally breaks** the template: use the brightest orange-to-yellow mix, so a milestone looks different in the subscription feed.
- **Focal point:** the logo or mascot centered. The label sits on the lower third.
- **Voice check:** state the fact plainly. Nothing like "HUGE NEWS!!!"

## (d) Scoring rubric (rate each existing thumbnail 1–5; total out of 30)

| # | Criterion | 1 = | 5 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Glance test (mobile)**: shrink to 160×90 px. Can you tell what it is in about 1 second? | mush | the subject and hook both read instantly |
| 2 | **Single focal point** | 4+ competing elements | 1–3 elements, clear hierarchy |
| 3 | **Text economy** | 6+ words, or repeats the title | 0–3 words that add something the title doesn't |
| 4 | **Curiosity gap with accuracy** | either nothing to wonder about or misleading | an honest open question the video answers in the first minute |
| 5 | **Brand recognition**: would someone know it's GOB with the logo covered? | generic | palette, type, and series mark are unmistakable |
| 6 | **Niche signal**: does it read as a deep basketball sim to FM/OOTP/2K fans? | could be any game | a grade, banner, or UI immediately signals "management sim" |

Score ≥24: keep. 18–23: redo it and A/B test. <18: rebuild using a formula from (c). Also check each video's CTR together with average view duration in Studio. YouTube's benchmark is that half of channels and videos fall between 2% and 10% impressions CTR ([source](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). Compare within your own channel over time, not against other channels.

## (e) Testing plan: YouTube Test & Compare

**How it works ([YT Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)):** desktop Studio only, Advanced Features required, eligible public long-form videos only. Up to 3 thumbnails, titles, or title+thumbnail pairs, shown at the same time to different viewers. The winner is decided by watch time share. Results come back as Winner, Performed Same, or Inconclusive (if inconclusive, the first upload stays). Upload at 1280×720 or higher. Changing the title or thumbnail during a test stops it. YouTube's tip: make variants **clearly different**, because near-duplicates drag the test out. Tests can take up to about 2 weeks depending on impressions.

**Plan:**
1. **Test one hypothesis per video, with big contrasts.** Suggested order:
   - (1) Jamie's face vs. no face (grade or artifact as the hero)
   - (2) 0 words vs. 3 words
   - (3) a letter grade as the hero vs. a UI screenshot
   - (4) Coach Sammy vs. Jamie
   - (5) orange vs. yellow accent
2. **Variant A is always the safest on-brand option**, since it stays as the default if the result is inconclusive.
3. **Run tests from upload on new videos**, and also on 2–3 older back-catalog videos that score below 18 on the rubric (YouTube recommends refreshing old thumbnails).
4. **Keep a log** (one spreadsheet row per test): hypothesis, variants, result type, watch time share split, days to finish. A low-traffic channel will see a lot of "Inconclusive" results. After 3 inconclusive results on the same variable, stop testing it and go with judgment.
5. **Confirm with wishlists, not just watch time.** Put a tracked Steam link (UTM or Steam's link tracking) first in the description. Each month, check Steam wishlist adds from YouTube against which format won.
6. **Review quarterly:** update formulas (c) and the 70/30 template based on 3+ consistent wins. Keep the roughly 1-in-4 "break the template" experiment slot.
