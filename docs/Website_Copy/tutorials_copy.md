## Player Attributes Tab

### Summary (header above dropdowns)

Player Attributes are the foundation of GOB. Every possession begins with individual ability — skill, instinct, and execution in the moment.

Over the course of a season, your players will evolve. Training emphasis, in-game decisions, and role assignments all shape their growth. Develop them intentionally, and they'll define your ceiling.

### Column 1 — Dropdowns

**Scoring (SC)**

Simply put, this is a player's ability to put the ball in the hoop. A high SC score indicates a player's ability to utilize post moves and adjust his shot to score in high-traffic areas. The closer the shot is to the basket, the more relevant this trait will be.

**Shooting (SH)**

You guessed it! This is a player's ability to shoot the rock. It's most important on outside shots — particularly 3-pointers. The further away a player's shot is from the basket, the more relevant this trait will be.

**Passing (PS)**

This is a player's ability to make good fundamental passes. A higher passing rating means fewer turnovers as well as a better pass being delivered to a potential shooter, which will increase the effectiveness of his shot.

**Ball Handling (BH)**

This trait is all about dribbling and handling the ball. A high BH score indicates a player who is less likely to turn the ball over and more effective when driving to the basket. Clearly this is most important for point guards and players who attack the hoop.

**Agility (AG)**

An indication of a player's foot speed. A high AG score will be very important when driving to the basket on offense, playing perimeter defense and covering a lot of ground as a zone defender.

**Endurance (ND)**

This represents a player's conditioning and willingness to fight through fatigue. Players with high ND scores will tire less. As a player's energy level erodes during gameplay, you'll notice he loses effectiveness in all traits across the board.

**Emotion (EM)**

😎 happiest  
😊 happy  
😐 neutral  
🙁 unhappy  
🤢 disruptive

### Column 2 — Dropdowns

**Inside Defense (ID)**

The ability to defend near and around the basket. The closer the opponent's shot is to the hoop, the more relevant this trait is. Taller players with high ID scores will establish themselves as "rim protectors" and block more shots.

**Outside Defense (OD)**

This trait represents a player's ability to guard the perimeter. Outside shots and drives to the hoop are better guarded by players with a high OD score. Also, it's indicative of a player's ability to reduce the effectiveness of passes and create turnovers.

**Rebounding (RB)**

This trait represents a player's technical know-how when fighting for rebounds. A high RB score means a player is proficient at reading missed shots and getting into good rebounding position. It also indicates a player's ability to box out an opponent.

**Strength (ST)**

This is the measure of how strong a player is. This trait comes into play when attempting to score or defend near the basket, fighting for a good shot when driving to the hoop, when rebounding, and when setting or fighting through screens.

**Basketball IQ (IQ)**

Basketball IQ is the closest thing you'll get to understanding a player's intangibles. Players who score high in this trait do many of the little things well. Getting teammates involved, making clutch plays and committing fewer fouls are just a few examples.

**Free Throws (FT)**

Exactly what you would expect. This trait indicates a player's ability at the charity stripe. Note this is a different skill than SH as it requires a different type of technical discipline and mental focus.

**Momentum (MO)**

Strictly an in-game measure. Players start each game with 0 momentum, and the value toggles up or down based on the flow of the game, their team's performance, and their individual performance. Timeouts and quarter breaks will bring each player's momentum closer to equilibrium. Valuable to reset your cold players and slow down your opponent's hot players.

## Team Attributes Tab

### Summary (header above dropdowns)

Team Attributes represent your program's identity over time. They reflect how your team plays, how it responds to adversity, and how well it sustains its style under pressure.

Similar to Player Attributes, these traits evolve throughout the season. Winning builds confidence and cohesion. Predictability erodes sharpness. Your training emphasis, strategic diversity, and in-game performance all shape the DNA of your team.

### Column 1 — Dropdowns

**Shooting**

Represents your team's collective confidence and rhythm as shooters. High Shooting teams trust their spacing, step into open looks decisively, and maintain composure when shots aren't falling early. Success fuels it. Poor selection and cold stretches can slowly chip away at it.

**Offense**

Reflects how effectively your team executes its offensive structure before the shot is taken. Ball movement, spacing, timing, and decision-making all flow through this attribute. Running varied schemes sharpens it. Becoming predictable puts it on tape — and makes execution harder.

Offense and Shooting work together. One creates the opportunity. The other converts it.

**Fast Breaks**

Determines how efficiently your team turns defense into immediate offense. Outlet decisions, lane discipline, and transition awareness all contribute. Teams that push with intention sharpen this trait. Reckless transition play can blunt it over time.

**Fight**

Represents your team's competitive edge. High Fight teams embrace physical play, respond to runs with resilience, and perform with urgency when trailing. Winning builds it. Adversity tests it. This trait defines how your team responds when the game turns hostile.

**Momentum**

An in-game amplifier. Momentum builds during strong stretches and can temporarily lift collective performance. While individual players may get "hot," team momentum affects the group as a whole. Home teams tend to generate it more easily, but sustained execution is what keeps it alive.

**Fast Break Defense**

Determines how quickly and effectively your team regains structure in transition. Stopping the ball, identifying matchups, and protecting the rim all matter. Teams that prioritize transition awareness see this grow over time.

### Column 2 — Dropdowns

**Rebounding**

Measures how consistently your team finishes possessions. Strong Rebounding teams anticipate misses, secure position early, and treat loose balls as momentum plays. Over time, disciplined fundamentals and physical commitment strengthen this trait — while lapses in effort weaken it.

**Defense**

Captures your team's cohesion and discipline on the defensive end. Rotations, help positioning, communication, and overall structure are all influenced here. Teams that consistently defend with purpose see this grow. Breakdowns and careless play gradually erode it.

**Press/Trap**

Measures your team's ability to apply structured pressure. Timing, coordination, and controlled aggression all matter. Repeated, predictable pressure schemes may lose effectiveness as opponents adjust. Strategic variety keeps this sharp.

**Discipline**

Reflects polish and control. Disciplined teams commit fewer unnecessary fouls and turnovers, execute aggressive strategies with precision, and maintain composure late in games. It balances Fight — aggression without structure becomes chaos.

**Team Chemistry**

The connective tissue of your roster. Chemistry influences how well players support one another through mistakes, adversity, and high-pressure moments. Winning strengthens it. Internal friction and extended losing can strain it. You may not see it directly — but you will feel it in tight games.

**Press/Trap Breaks**

Measures your team's composure under pressure. Spacing discipline, passing clarity, and decision-making against traps all feed this attribute. Teams that regularly face and solve pressure become sharper. Panic erodes it.

## Game Plans Tab

No summary. Dropdowns only.

### Column 1 — Dropdowns

**Offense**

Determines your mix of motion offense vs structured set plays. Leaning toward motion increases read-and-react freedom. Leaning toward sets emphasizes designed execution and called actions.

**Inside**

Controls how frequently you prioritize inside scoring. This influences both the number of Inside Set Plays you call and how aggressively your team looks to establish paint touches within motion offense. All things being equal, inside shots tend to be your most high-percentage shots.

**Attack**

Controls how often your team looks to generate attack shots — drives, cuts, and downhill pressure. A higher setting increases Attack Set Plays and pushes motion offense toward rim pressure. Attack shots tend to draw the most defensive fouls, and are best executed by your most athletic players.

**Outside**

Controls how frequently your team prioritizes perimeter scoring. This affects both Outside Set Plays and your shot selection tendencies within motion offense. Outside shots are your lowest percentage shots, but they tend to have the most impact on the game.

**Fast Breaks**

Determines how aggressively your team pushes in transition. Higher settings increase fast break attempts. Lower settings favor entering structured half-court offense.

### Column 2 — Dropdowns

**Defense**

Determines your mix of man-to-man vs zone defense. Adjusting this shifts how your team protects the rim, contests perimeter shots, and handles defensive rotations.

Man defense generates more individual matchups and high-impact plays. It's ideal if you have disciplined defenders who can win their assignments and recover quickly.

Zone defense offers structural advantages. Players fatigue less, commit fewer fouls, and receive more consistent help support. However, zones are more vulnerable to perimeter shooting and can concede offensive rebounds due to the lack of fixed box-out assignments. Zone schemes are also executed more effectively by high-IQ defenders who anticipate and rotate instinctively.

**Aggression**

Controls how aggressively your team attempts to create disruption. Higher aggression increases steal attempts and playmaking risks across the floor. It also increases fatigue rate and foul likelihood.

**Half-Court Trap**

Determines how frequently your team executes half-court traps. Trapping increases turnover potential and can disrupt offensive chemistry, but defenders fatigue faster and are more prone to fouls.

**Full-Court Press**

Determines how frequently your team applies full-court pressure. Presses amplify turnover potential and disruption more than half-court traps, but also carry greater fatigue cost and foul risk.

**Rebounding**

Determines how aggressively your team attacks the offensive glass. Higher settings send more players to crash for second-chance opportunities. Lower settings prioritize defensive balance and transition coverage.

## Playbooks Tab

Includes: two dropdowns (headers above image), then image (`/images/training-playcall-offense.png`).

**Playcall Distribution**

Determines how frequently each play type is called within your overall offensive system. These are the micro-level decisions layered on top of your macro Game Plan settings (Motion vs Set Plays, Inside vs Attack vs Outside).

Your distribution settings shape the rhythm of your offense — influencing which actions appear most often over the course of a game.

**Playcall Center Settings**

Select up to six plays to feature in your Playcall Center during live gameplay. These are the actions immediately available when you want to choose a specific play.

Your Playcall Center defines your go-to options in key moments. Choose a balanced mix, or load it with situational weapons — the decision is yours.

## Training Tab

### Column 1 — Dropdowns

**Player Drills & General**

Focused drills designed to improve specific player attributes. Most drills directly enhance the attribute listed in the header, with a few key exceptions:

Conditioning improves Endurance (ND).

Film Study improves Basketball IQ (IQ) and gives players better insight into upcoming opponents — especially tendencies from their most recent game.

Breaks enhance the overall effectiveness of all drills (Player, Team, and Scrimmages).

Breaks also reduce fatigue heading into the next game. However, too many breaks can strain team chemistry and weaken Fight and Discipline. Teams with strong chemistry can absorb more downtime without losing edge.

**Coaching Style**

This is where you define your identity.

Your chosen coaching focus determines what you emphasize during drills, scrimmages, post-practice wrap-ups, and pre-game locker room talks. It shapes motivation, preparation tone, and the areas where your team sees the most growth. For maximum impact, your coaching focus should align with the drills you emphasize that week.

Leaning heavily into one primary category builds a clear, recognizable identity. Pairing it with a strong secondary focus can create compounded gains as the season progresses. The style you choose each week influences not just development — but who your team ultimately becomes.

### Column 2 — Dropdowns

**Team Drills**

Designed to improve Team Attributes directly. Each drill aligns explicitly with its category — Offense, Defense, Fast Breaks, Fast Break Defense, Press/Traps, and Press/Trap Breaks.

Consistent, targeted emphasis strengthens these traits over time. Neglect allows them to erode.

**Scrimmages**

Where preparation becomes application. Scrimmages sharpen execution, improve overall readiness, and reinforce system cohesion.

More scrimmaging generally leads to better game preparedness. However, excessive scrimmages increase fatigue and can strain team chemistry. Depending on how your team responds, chemistry may improve through competitive bonding — or decline under pressure.
